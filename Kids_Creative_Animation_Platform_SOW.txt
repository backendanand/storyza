Scope of Work (SOW)
Version 1.0 | Date: 30 August 2026
1. Executive Summary
This Scope of Work defines the first-year delivery scope for a school-focused Creative Learning Platform that enables children to create animations, stories and digital projects through a simple, guided interface. The platform is designed for classroom use, with teachers assigning activities and reviewing student work.
The Year-1 objective is not to build a professional animation suite. It is to launch a commercially usable, child-friendly 2D creative platform within 12 months, validate adoption with schools, and establish a foundation for AI-assisted creation, 3D, games and XR over subsequent years.
2. Product Vision
Enable children to turn ideas into digital experiences—starting with animation—while helping schools integrate creativity, storytelling and digital creation into everyday learning.
3. Year-1 Business Objective
Deliver a production-ready web/tablet platform within 12 months.
Support classroom workflows: school → class → activity → assignment → student creation → submission → teacher review.
Validate the product with real school pilots during development rather than waiting for final launch.
Establish a subscription/license-ready B2B/B2B2C product model.
Build the platform so AI can be introduced without redesigning the core product.

No unrestricted public chat or open student-to-stranger communication.
6. Technical Scope
7. High-Level Architecture
The platform will use a web-first architecture. React handles the product UI while PixiJS handles the interactive 2D creative canvas. FastAPI provides business APIs. PostgreSQL stores transactional data and JSONB project documents. Object storage stores media/assets. Celery workers process exports, media and AI jobs asynchronously. Redis provides caching and task-broker capabilities.
Animation projects will use a versioned JSON document model so that the same project can later be consumed by advanced renderers, AI services, 3D workflows or XR experiences.

11. Success Criteria
A target Class 3–6 student can create a simple 15–30 second animation with minimal adult assistance.
A teacher can create/assign an activity and review a submission without technical support.
A school can onboard users/classes and operate classroom workflows.
Projects autosave reliably and can be recovered.
Exports complete asynchronously without blocking API requests.
Tenant and role boundaries are enforced.
Pilot schools demonstrate repeated use rather than one-time experimentation.
The platform is ready for paid licensing after pilot validation.
12. Assumptions & Dependencies
Initial target is web/tablet rather than native mobile.
Year-1 curriculum content will be curated and age-appropriate.
AI provider costs and policies may change; provider abstraction is required.
School onboarding and pilot feedback are essential project dependencies.
Content moderation and child-safety requirements must be validated for target markets before commercial rollout.
